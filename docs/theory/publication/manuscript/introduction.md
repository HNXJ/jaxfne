# Introduction

Neural simulation is a standard tool for connecting mechanistic hypotheses about neural circuits to observable signals, and a single study now commonly spans several levels at once: spiking dynamics are simulated, extracellular potentials are computed from the simulated sources, and summary readouts are derived for comparison with experimental recordings [1,2]. Recent simulation toolchains built on automatic differentiation and GPU acceleration have made it possible to fit and train such models with gradient-based optimization [3,4,5], while established general-purpose simulators remain the standard infrastructure for dynamics-oriented modelling [6,7,8]. Together, these systems cover a broad space of modelling practice, from point-neuron populations to morphologically detailed cells and whole-brain networks.

Across these toolchains, however, the relationships between pipeline stages are established by per-tool convention rather than by explicit contract. Whether readouts are derived from a single frozen trajectory or from separate machinery; whether a reported quantity is a relative computational readout or a calibrated physical measurement; whether an internal state that is not part of the observable output can nevertheless influence it; and which pathway of a recurrent circuit owns a given connection — a user must recover each of these properties from documentation or code. Readout operators have a long history in differentiable formulations of spiking networks [9], and observation chains attached to one simulation appear in several systems [1,2]; but no surveyed framework exposes these stage boundaries as typed, verifiable elements of the simulation itself.

This opacity has consequences for the credibility of published simulation studies. Verification practices increasingly ask that reported results be obtainable by independent parties following the described procedures, and that results be checked against plans registered before the outcomes were known [10,11]; reproducibility standards for computational methods likewise stress explicit, checkable pipelines [12]. When the provenance of every reported quantity is not auditable, the boundary of every claim — what was computed, from what, and under which assumptions — is correspondingly difficult to verify.

We describe a simulator architecture that makes these relationships explicit. The pipeline is factorized into typed stages, each carrying an explicit ownership and disablement contract: a dynamics stage that evolves the neural state; a source stage that produces a canonical relative source from that state; a field stage that maps the source onto field coordinates; and a probe stage that derives readouts. Observations are pure downstream compositions of a single frozen source: they never require re-simulation and never feed back into the simulated dynamics. Written internal state is typed and can be kept computationally latent — when its coupling map is disabled, the observable output is bit-identical to a run without the state. Quantities are declared as relative or calibrated as part of the pipeline contract, so that every reported value carries its measurement semantics with it.

The implementation, jaxfne, is JAX-native. Each extension of the classical emitter is defined by a reduction identity: setting the written state to its neutral value restores the base dynamics bit-for-bit; removing pathway delays recovers the instantaneous pipeline; disabling observations recovers the substrate state exactly. These identities make the boundary of every extension testable by construction.

The framework is validated on its own pipeline rather than on claims of biological fidelity. A traveling-wave estimator — a widely studied signature of propagating activity [13,14] — is validated on synthetic controls only, and a preregistered grid of recurrent geometries and delay policies yields no traveling-wave classification in the tested regimes, a negative outcome reported as such. A candidate adaptation phenotype — spike-count attenuation across repeated stimulation — is not supported: attenuation cannot be attributed to the written state, and the mechanism gates of the formal classification are not satisfied; the outcome is reported as such. The propagation of a written state through an existing hierarchical pathway is demonstrated with bit-exact leakage controls; and a region of closed-loop dynamics remains actively unresolved rather than being converted into a negative result. All main figures are rendered from a single frozen evidence set with per-file hashes, and the figure pipeline is reproducible from a clean checkout.

Our contribution is a simulation framework whose stage boundaries, state semantics, and claim boundaries are explicit and checkable, demonstrated through registered, deterministic validation — including negative and unresolved outcomes reported as such — rather than through comparisons of simulation speed, fidelity, or training capability.
