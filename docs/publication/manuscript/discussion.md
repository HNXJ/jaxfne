# Discussion

## Principal methodological contribution

The framework contributes a formalism for making claim boundaries in neural simulation explicit, and an implementation that demonstrates the formalism through registered, deterministic validation. The formalism factorizes the simulation pipeline into typed stages — dynamics, source, field, probe — where each stage boundary carries an explicit ownership and disablement contract, and where the semantics of every reported quantity (relative or calibrated) is part of the pipeline contract rather than a convention recovered from documentation [1,2]. The implementation, jaxfne, is JAX-native, building on the established capability of differentiable simulation for fitting and training neural models [3,4,5] and on the mature infrastructure of general-purpose simulators for dynamics-oriented modelling [6,7,8]; the contribution is not automatic differentiation itself, and we do not claim that any single pipeline stage is individually new. What is distinctive is the contract architecture: every extension of the classical emitter is defined by a reduction identity — restoring the neutral written state, removing pathway delays, or disabling observations recovers the base semantics bit-for-bit — so that the boundary of each extension is a tested numerical property rather than a design promise. Written state that is not part of the observable output can be kept computationally latent: with its coupling map disabled, the observable output is bit-identical to a run without the state. Because observations are pure downstream compositions of a single frozen trajectory, every readout in this paper derives from one executed simulation per seed, and no downstream estimator can feed back into the simulated dynamics.

## What the experiments reveal

The same executable framework produced supported, negative, and unresolved outcomes, each reported as such. A traveling-wave estimator — the operationalization of a widely studied cortical signature [13,14] — had its validity established on synthetic controls only, including recovery of all 48 synthetic traveling waves and rejection of all five negative control families. Against that synthetic-validated detector, a preregistered grid of recurrent geometries and delay policies produced no traveling-wave classification in the tested regimes, a predeclared bounded search over a delay family produced the predeclared outcome "no positive domain in the tested range", and an embedding analysis showed that a genuine in-band wave of relative amplitude equal to the measured field structure is not hidden by that structure: classification flips at relative amplitude 1.0 on all three tested cells. These results do not imply that networks of this class cannot generate traveling waves: the grid's delay policies collapsed onto a single effective delay, and the search family ran under near-silent activity, so no tested combination of active dynamics and genuinely heterogeneous delays was available. The outcome is a bounded negative result — not a statement about untested regimes, and not a neural positive control. The estimator's own limits were characterized rather than assumed: at even site counts a single-frequency phasor method cannot distinguish standing from traveling structure at the reason level, and detection is parity-dependent at low site counts — constraints on the instrument, not on the network.

A candidate adaptation phenotype — spike-count attenuation across repeated stimulation — was tested under mechanism gates that require both the observable and the written state to move together. Attenuation was observable across all arms, and it was not attributable to the written state: the null arm was indistinguishable from the full arm, and the formal classification returned NO_ADAPTATION in every cell of the written-state arm. The outcome is reported as attenuation, not adaptation. A decoded-memory assay found no positive length effect by the preregistered test, and the exploratory point estimate it produced is reported without bootstrap confidence intervals. In contrast, the propagation of a written state through an existing hierarchical feedback pathway was demonstrated with bit-exact leakage controls across all seeds. A region of closed-loop hidden-state dynamics remains unresolved: of the 2187 tested lattice points, none were classified robustly stable and none unstable, while 1944 active points remain classified as stability-unresolved; the region is reported as unresolved rather than converted into a negative result.

Together, a positive supports a mechanism when its causal link holds, a negative marks a failed link, and an unresolved result marks missing discriminating evidence. Typed H_K expression drives hierarchical propagation (E5); geometry and delay alone did not create a traveling wave (F5); visible attenuation alone did not meet the adaptation gates (D3); active HDP stability and bounded-but-not-proven-stable trajectories (W3b) remain open; and relative field proxies show structure without claiming physical calibration. Each boundary is a checked property of the implementation, not a design promise.

## Interpretation relative to existing methods

The framework is intended to be used alongside, not instead of, established tools. General-purpose simulators remain the standard infrastructure for dynamics-oriented modelling [6,7,8], and differentiable toolchains have opened gradient-based fitting of detailed and whole-brain models [3,4,5]. What the surveyed frameworks do not expose is the typed contract layer this paper demonstrates: stage boundaries, state semantics, and claim provenance as verifiable elements of the simulation itself. In practice the framework is designed to be complementary: the contract separates concerns so that a dynamics stage could be replaced by a mature simulator's kernel without altering the source/field/probe contract, and a calibrated bridge could later attach physical units to the relative readouts; neither replacement is demonstrated here, and no claim that these systems are superseded.

## Biological and physical claim boundaries

Every readout reported here is a relative computational readout produced by proxy operators. The field operators are linear projection/proxy operators, not volume-conductor or partial-differential-equation solves, and no physical amplitude calibration was performed; readouts therefore carry relative structure, not absolute or unit-bearing magnitudes, and cannot be compared in amplitude to experimental recordings. The written hidden state is a relative coordinate within the declared observable chain: its dynamics are implementation-bounded by hard clamps that guarantee finiteness, which is not dynamical stability. In the executed runs the tested trajectories remained bounded over the tested domain; the default configuration applies no active restoration to connection magnitudes, so boundedness or stability over untested horizons is not established, and the region of closed-loop dynamics that remains unresolved is exactly where no stability claim is made. Observed reduced-model behavior — attenuation, memory-like decoding, propagation — is compatible with biological phenomena of the same name, and is not evidence of biological mechanism identity; computational compatibility is not biological validation, and no experimental recordings were used or reproduced. How much spike-associated versus slower transmembrane currents contribute to the modeled field under specified geometry and filtering remains a generic quantitative question; no percentage is claimed here pending quantified evidence (quarantined prior ~4% statement removed).

## Limitations

The scope of the paper is bounded in ways that each carry a consequence. Readouts are relative proxies: only their relative structure is claimed, and no absolute comparison with experimental recordings is possible. The tested domain is finite — one ring geometry, a single reduced emitter family, fixed connection magnitudes — and no generalization to other architectures, cell types, or scales is claimed. All runs were deterministic; seed-level consistency was demonstrated over fixed seed sets, while noise robustness, and any vocabulary built on it, is not established. The estimator's standing-gate parity limits standing-versus-traveling discrimination at the reason level, so reason-level assignments are findings rather than gates, with classification as the backstop. The absence of a neural positive wave domain reflects the tested combinations: active dynamics and genuinely heterogeneous delays were never jointly available, so the wave question is deferred to domains with adequate activity rather than answered negatively.

The evidence itself carries three further bounds. Hidden-state boundedness is implementation-level: hard clamps guarantee finiteness, and the unresolved closed-loop region is left unresolved, so no stability property is claimed for it. The secondary validation runs were executed after the primary registration in response to review; they are reported separately and strengthen the estimator and domain statements without altering the registered claims. And the exploratory memory point estimate lacks confidence intervals, so the memory conclusion is the absence of a detected positive effect, not evidence of no effect.

## Future directions

The limitations delimit the next steps. Calibrated source bridges would attach physical units to the relative source and readouts; validated field solvers would replace the proxy projections with physical solves; and gradient-based inverse problems over the typed pipeline would connect the framework to empirical recordings. Broader dynamical families — conductance-based and morphologically detailed emitters — would extend the tested domain, and searching regimes with detectable wave activity would revisit the delay question under adequate activity. Concretely, a prospective wave-capable regime would test a spatially structured recurrent/excitable mechanism in which local activity regenerates from site to site — schematically $M_{\rm wave}=0\Rightarrow\mathrm{NO\_WAVE}$ and $M_{\rm wave}=1\Rightarrow\mathrm{TRAVELING\_WAVE}$ — and would map transitions among extinction, synchrony, standing/flipping, and traveling propagation as a function of propagation gain, recovery (with u ∈ X, not H), coupling range and asymmetry, and conduction velocity. A preregistered recovery-clock battery further distinguishes H_clock (period T0 tracks recovery timescale τ_u=1/a_E with slope within 30% and Spearman ρ≥0.40, regime preserved: ρ_volley within 0.20, CV_T≤0.35, rates within 2×) from H_excit (T0 shift via excitability/rate change, regime collapse → UNRESOLVED_MECHANISM); this battery is deferred to a separately staged execution (e2_c_recovery_clock_prereg.v2, spec_hash b7840069) and is not performed here. This experiment is not performed in the present study; it is a deliberately scoped future intervention whose outcome is not assumed. Further ahead, a developmental operator could generate a realized circuit specification upstream of the existing pipeline, so that constructed architectures themselves become objects of study; this is a future possibility, not a capability of the present work. Each direction is downstream of a stated limitation, and each preserves the property that motivated the framework: every claim remains checkable by construction.
