## Goal and falsifier

Question: Is learned multiplicative interaction sufficient and necessary for efficient spatially constrained relational computation in the authors' declared regime? Does adding oscillatory phase structure or physically grounded extracellular coupling improve held-out relational performance or wiring efficiency beyond capacity-matched alternatives?

Falsifiers: (a) multiplicative mechanism fails to outperform nonlinear additive controls under matched parameters/wiring/training; (b) phase perturbations do not alter relational performance when rate and input statistics are controlled; (c) field-feedback removal leaves dynamics and performance unchanged, or the claimed effect vanishes under physically plausible field strengths.

## Stage O — oscillatory extension (hypothesis)

Start with the smallest rate/phase model, not a full spiking network:

  tau_x dx_i/dt = -x_i + f(u_i + sum_j w_ij x_j(t-d_ij) + g_i(t))
  dtheta_i/dt = omega_i + sum_j kappa_ij sin(theta_j(t-d_ij)-theta_i(t))
  a_ij(t) = softmax_j(q_i^T k_j / sqrt(d) + beta cos(theta_i-theta_j))

x_i: activity (declared dimensionless or rate); u_i: input; tau_x,d_ij: time constants/delays (s); w_ij: dimensionless after activity normalization; g_i: input units matching x_i; theta_i: phase (rad); omega_i: rad/s; kappa_ij: 1/s; q_i,k_j: learned dimensionless embeddings; d: embedding dimension; beta: dimensionless phase-gating strength. This is a phenomenological hypothesis, not a derivation of attention from oscillations.

Controls: beta=0; phase shuffle with preserved power; matched frequency/power without interpopulation coherence; delay perturbation; matched activity distributions; oscillator without learned multiplicative weights. Estimands: held-out relational accuracy/loss, wiring budget, latency, robustness, phase-response dependence. Test whether phase effects survive control for altered firing rates and overall gain. Oscillations may help, harm or be irrelevant.

## Stage E — electrical/biophysical extension (hypothesis)

Distinguish:
1. Synaptic electrical currents already driving X (do not double count).
2. Extracellular potential Phi generated by physical transmembrane sources Q under conductivity and boundary assumptions.
3. Causal ephaptic feedback into extended membrane geometry; extracellular potential alone is not membrane polarization.
4. Ion concentration/electrodiffusion as a separate, slower mechanism; not a synonym for extracellular voltage.

For an explicitly calibrated volume conductor:
  div(sigma grad Phi) = q_s  (source sign convention specified).
  E = -grad Phi.
  C_m dV_m/dt = I_injected - I_ion - I_syn + I_field
where I_field is derived from a declared compartmental extracellular-potential difference/cable coupling, not arbitrarily proportional to Phi. sigma in S/m, Phi in V, E in V/m, q_s in A/m^3, C_m in F, V_m in V, currents in A. Geometry, reference/gauge, boundary conditions and units are mandatory.

JaxFNE default relative field proxies cannot be used to claim physical ephaptic efficacy. The experimental 1-D Poisson route is not an externally calibrated cortical field instrument. Before any feedback experiment validate analytic/manufactured source fields and compartment polarization, and compare against a zero-field and spatially uniform-potential control. Include coupling-strength sweeps bounded by independently sourced physical ranges; do not choose strength by task performance.

## Decision structure

A positive O result establishes an oscillation-dependent computational benefit in the tested model, not cortical oscillatory implementation. A positive E result establishes causal model-level field feedback only if physical calibration and controls pass; otherwise it is a relative/proxy experiment. Mechanistic biological claims require independent electrophysiology and perturbations.
